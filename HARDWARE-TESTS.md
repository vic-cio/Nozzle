# Hardware test record

Only observations made on the physical printer belong here. Simulator results and
assumptions are deliberately kept separate.

## 2026-08-10 — first complete print

### Result reported by Victor

- The first print started from Nozzle's Print UI and completed successfully.
- The finished part was solid and had the expected proportions.
- No physical fault or visibly corrupted movement was reported.

### Evidence available after the print

- Nozzle's temperature history showed the nozzle and bed holding approximately 210 °C
  and 50 °C during the print, followed by a normal cooldown.
- The surviving Console slice showed numbered G-code commands receiving paired `ok`
  acknowledgements and a readable `M105` reply at 210/210 °C nozzle and 50/50 °C bed.
- macOS recorded no Nozzle crash report and no application-specific printer/serial fault.
  Its error-severity entries were unrelated App Intents, Spotlight and text-input service
  messages generated by macOS.
- Victor intentionally disconnected the printer after the completed print. The resulting
  Disconnected state and absent serial port were expected, not an application, USB or
  in-print failure.

### Verification boundary

- **Milestone 3 is hardware-verified end to end:** a real Cura G-code file was loaded,
  validated, streamed and completed as a correctly proportioned physical part.
- The print also exercised firmware homing, heating, coordinated motion and extrusion,
  but it does not by itself verify every interactive Prepare/Temperatures control in
  Milestone 2.
- The hardware temperature graph displayed real print/cooldown data. Pause, Resume, Stop
  during a print, sleep prevention and a long pause remain unverified for Milestone 4.
- No dimensional measurement was recorded, so “expected proportions” is qualitative and
  must not be rewritten as calibrated dimensional accuracy.

### Logging limitation found

The Console retained its maximum 5,000 lines and reported 25,594 earlier lines dropped.
It is an in-memory buffer, not a durable print log, so a complete transcript was not
available after the session ended. Persistent bounded per-print logs are now part of the
Milestone 5 draft.

## 2026-08-11 — hot-plug detection

### Result reported by Victor

Nozzle was launched with the printer unplugged, and the USB cable was then connected.
The port appeared in the picker without Refresh being pressed. Victor then unplugged it
while disconnected and confirmed the entry disappeared. Reported simply as working; no
fault, delay or unwanted connection attempt was mentioned.

### Verification boundary

- Both halves of `SerialPortMonitor` — attach and detach — are hardware-verified on this
  Mac with this printer's CH340 adapter.
- The absence of an automatic connection was not separately confirmed, though an
  unasked-for connection during either test would have been obvious in the status bar.
- One Mac, one adapter. Nothing here proves the timing on a different USB chipset.

## 2026-08-11 — failed print, stopped by the user

### What happened

A small flat oval tag with raised lettering, sliced at 0.16 mm layer height, failed on its
first layer. Victor stopped it from Nozzle. Photograph: `~/Downloads/IMG_4963.png`,
12:41.

### What the photograph shows

- First-layer beads are round and translucent rather than flattened and fused — the
  signature of too large a nozzle-to-bed gap.
- Loose tangled strands where the lettering should be: extrusion that never bonded, then
  dragged by later passes.
- The bed surface looks smeared, with ghost outlines from earlier prints.

### Cause, and what it is not

Read as a first-layer adhesion failure: bed gap, bed cleanliness, or both. **Not** caused
by the 0.16 mm layer height, and not attributable to Nozzle — a streaming fault looks like
blobs, stalls or resends, none of which are visible here. The one contribution the fine
layer height could have made is margin: if Cura's *initial* layer height followed the
global value down to 0.16 mm, a given levelling error costs proportionally more.

Nothing was checked in the serial log. The Console is an in-memory 5,000-line buffer, and
by the time the failure was discussed the print traffic had probably aged out behind
`M105` polling. This is the second time a print's log has been wanted after the fact and
not been there — see the logging limitation from 2026-08-10, and the persistent per-print
log in the Milestone 5 draft.

### Remedies applied, outcome not yet reported

Bed cleaned, layer height raised to 0.2 mm, bed re-levelled with the paper method using
A4 copier paper (~0.1 mm). No reprint result recorded yet.

## 2026-08-12 — 3DBenchy, successful

### Result reported by Victor

A 3DBenchy printed to completion and came out clean. Photograph: `3D_Benchy.png` in the
project root. Recorded as streamed from Nozzle, as every print in this session has been.

Victor's own assessment: clean overall, a few rough patches on the undersides of the
arches. During the build he saw strands occasionally curl up, and each time the nozzle
flattened them on the following pass without the print degrading.

### What the photograph shows

- Even layer lines up the full height of the hull. No layer shift, no warping, no
  under-extrusion gaps, no separation.
- The chimney — a small-radius cylinder, the feature most likely to show wobble — is
  round and true.
- Roughness confined to overhang undersides, the expected cooling limitation.
- Fine stringing across the cabin and window openings, and small raised specks on the
  cabin roof consistent with strings ironed flat by later passes.

### Why this one matters

Benchy is by a wide margin the densest command stream Nozzle has streamed: many short
segments, constant retractions, curved walls. A host that could not keep the printer's
motion buffer fed would show it here as blobs, stutter marks or scattered seams. None are
visible, and Victor reported no stall. This is the strongest evidence so far that the
send-and-wait loop at `maxInFlightCommands = 1` keeps up with real slicer output at
0.2 mm.

### Verification boundary

- Not a controlled test of streaming throughput; it is one successful print, judged by
  eye from one photograph. Nothing here establishes a headroom figure.
- The setup that produced it — diluted PVA on the smooth sheet, 60 °C bed, re-levelled
  by hand — differs from the 2026-08-10 cube, so this does not isolate any single change.
- The seam bulges seen on 2026-08-11 stack vertically on the finished part, so they are
  Cura's layer seam, not the host stalling mid-move. Victor judged them minor and left
  them alone. One more reason to read the clean Benchy as evidence the streaming loop
  keeps up.
